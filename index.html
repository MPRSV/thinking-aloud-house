TA–7K4X–L9

TA–7K4X–L9

I encountered a single stem emerging from hardened molten lava in Hawaiʻi. It did not appear to be waiting for favorable conditions. It was simply growing where growth seemed improbable.

Conditions can explain difficulty without becoming the final authority on possibility.

TA–Q8R2–D4

TA–Q8R2–D4

Raindrops remained fixed to the vertical panel of my car despite gravity. Each held a different shape and size. All were rain.

Are they individual, collected, connected, or conforming?

TA–M3V7–E1

TA–M3V7–E1

A droplet balanced on the curvature of a thin leaf moving with the wind. The photograph cannot capture the motion surrounding it—or the fact that the droplet did not move.

Fragility and stability are not opposites.

TA–H5S9–T2

TA–H5S9–T2

On a crowded train at the end of the workday, a daughter rested completely against her father. For that moment, she trusted the person holding her enough to surrender awareness of everything else.

A rhododendron accompanied a public request for housing. A stranger answered with an almost identically framed rhododendron—and a home offering what I had described.

The Human
Intersection

PROPOSED INSTALLATION

The Human Intersection is a participatory installation that asks visitors to encounter lived experience before identity. Anonymous responses to shared human questions appear first; selected demographic context comes later, making the assumptions a visitor carried into the encounter part of the work. The goal is not to erase difference, but to make overlooked commonality visible.

    The Architect

    Najah Hull

    The shortest distance between A and B may be a straight line, but where’s the fun in that?!

    An ordinary observation has rarely remained ordinary for me. I may begin at A, but before I ever reach B, I’ve encountered G, K, S, D—and sometimes found my way back to A with an entirely different understanding of where I started.

    That interconnectivity is how much of my work begins. Something I notice becomes a question. One connection exposes another, then another, until what appeared to be an isolated observation reveals an entire architecture of thought.
